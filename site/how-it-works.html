How it works

From 500 alerts to one clear verdict.

No score. Four facts about the bug and your estate, a decision table you can read in a minute, and a sentence you can repeat to your boss. The AI, if you turn it on, explains the verdict. It never decides it.

Step one

Know what you run.

Connectors read your estate with read-only accounts and merge what they see into one asset list. Anything they cannot tie to a vendor's own vulnerability naming lands in a queue for a one-click decision, and that decision is remembered.

Servers and endpoints

Windows over WinRM or OpenSSH: build and hotfixes, installed software, roles and features, listening ports and which process owns them, IIS, SQL Server, .NET. Linux over SSH: packages and sockets. Or read what your management tool already knows: Intune, Defender for Endpoint, Configuration Manager, Jamf Pro, Kandji, NinjaOne, Datto RMM, N-central, Lansweeper, PDQ Connect or FortiClient EMS.

The network edge

FortiGate: firmware, switch and access-point firmware, and exposure for free. Which VIPs sit behind an internet-facing policy, whether the VPN or admin page listens on the WAN side. The same for Palo Alto, SonicWall, Sophos, Meraki MX, UniFi, pfSense and OPNsense: firmware, port forwards and whatever the firewall itself offers the internet. Zyxel adds its virtual servers; WatchGuard and Check Point Spark give firmware only, and the external cross-check covers the rest.

Everything else

vCenter and Hyper-V for the VM list, SNMP for printers, NAS, UPS and out-of-band controllers, SBOMs from your build pipeline, a discovery sweep for the hosts nobody claims, and an external cross-check of your public names.

Step two

Four inputs, all computed, none tuned.

Is it being exploited?

CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list, the vendor's own advisory, public exploit code in Exploit-DB, Metasploit or Nuclei, and the EPSS probability. Active, proof-of-concept, or none.

Can it be done at scale?

Not the CVSS score: the vector behind it. Over the network, no special conditions, no login, no user to trick. If all four hold, a bot can do it to everyone.

Where does it sit?

Internet-facing, internal only, isolated, or not installed at all. A local or physical attack path on an internet-facing box is treated as internal, because network exposure does not help that attacker.

How much does it matter?

Domain controllers, identity, firewalls and hypervisors are critical on sight. Lab and disposable kit is low. Everything else is standard.

Step three

A decision table, not a formula.

Sixteen rules, evaluated top to bottom, first match wins. Derived from the SSVC method published by CISA and Carnegie Mellon, simplified to what a one-person team needs. Every rule has a test. A few examples:

"Ignore, tracked" is re-evaluated every hour. When a public exploit appears or CISA adds it to the exploited list, it is promoted and the digest says so: "This one changed."

Step four

A sentence, and the evidence behind it.

FortiOS 7.2.5 on FW-EDGE-01: exploited in the wild, works over the network with no login, reachable from the internet. Fixed in 7.2.8.

Behind that sentence sits the chain that produced it, stored with every verdict, including the ones dismissed:

  1. Affected product Fortinet / FortiOS: vendor and product match exactly. CVE List V5, Fortinet's own record, retrieved 06:14.
  2. Version check: 7.2.5 is inside the affected range 7.2.0 to 7.2.7. Same record.
  3. FW-EDGE-01 runs FortiOS 7.2.5. FortiGate connector, seen 05:30.
  4. Listed in CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities since 9 February 2024, known ransomware use. CISA KEV, retrieved 06:11.
  5. CVSS 3.1 vector: network, low complexity, no privileges, no user interaction. Automatable.
  6. Reachable from the internet: SSL-VPN on wan1 port 443. FortiGate connector.
  7. Decision table rule 2: exploited in the wild and reachable from the internet, so Fix today.
Then it gets out of your way
  • Done, snooze, accept risk, suppress. With an owner and an expiry, so nothing is quietly forgotten.
  • Compensating controls. A WAF in front, MFA on the service, a feature switched off: one step down, named in the sentence. Never for "exploited and internet-facing".
  • Auto-close. When a connector sees the fixed version, the verdict closes itself and the digest says "patched, closed".
  • Tickets and webhooks. One ticket per Fix today or Fix this week, with a correlation key, into your helpdesk. Signed webhooks for anything else.
  • A weekly report. New, affecting us, actioned, overdue, dismissed. For the person who asks about the thing on the news.
And keeps your estate to itself
  • Runs in a container or on a VM in your network. Feeds come in; inventory never leaves.
  • Credentials encrypted at rest, read-only everywhere, never logged.
  • Only port 443 exposed. Content security policy, rate-limited login, audit log, single sign-on with your directory.
  • Signed feed bundles for air-gapped sites; the console refuses anything unsigned or older than what it has.
  • AI explanations are optional and BYOM, bring your own model: your Anthropic or OpenAI key, or a local model for sites that allow nothing out. The model never sees a hostname or an address.
